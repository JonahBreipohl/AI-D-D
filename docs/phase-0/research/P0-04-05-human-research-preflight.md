## Authority, purpose, and hard stop

This document converts the safeguards in P0-04 and P0-05 into a fieldwork gate. It is an operational draft, **not legal advice, an institutional review, or a substitute for qualified human review of the actual operator, tools, notices, and circumstances**.

No invitation may be sent, no candidate screened, and no research session begun until:

1. every common and study-applicable bracketed field or table cell marked `REQUIRED` has a truthful, exact value, and any study-specific `N/A` is allowed only under the authorization-scope rule below;
2. the accountable humans in the signoff register have reviewed the same frozen version and approved it in writing;
3. the storage, deletion, pause, stop, report, distress, and incident procedures have passed dry runs; and
4. the final go/no-go checklist is entirely `GO`.

Missing, ambiguous, expired, contradictory, or untested information means **NO-GO**. Schedule pressure, a participant's willingness, or an owner's verbal approval does not waive a stop condition. A10 owns the research halt; any staff member may invoke it.

This preflight does not complete P0-04 or P0-05. Those tasks require real, consented evidence under their separate completion rules.

Authorization is study-specific. The separate final blocks in section 14 authorize P0-04 and P0-05 independently. A P0-04-only `GO` does **not** authorize P0-05 recruitment, screening, scheduling, dry runs using participant data, or sessions. P0-05-only requirements may be marked `N/A` only in support of a P0-04-only decision, with a written rationale and human approver. A P0-05 `GO` requires every common and P0-05 requirement; it cannot rely on a P0-04-only approval.

All accountable roles and approvers in this document are **named humans**. AI systems, including Codex or any other agent, are advisory drafting and checking aids only. They cannot be A0, A5, A10, the human owner, a qualified reviewer, facilitator, interviewer, Wizard, rules/dice operator, auditor, observer, data owner, report recipient, safety contact, backup, or authorization signer. AI output cannot satisfy a named-human or qualified-review row, assess consent or capacity, monitor a participant or inbox, conduct fieldwork, respond to an incident, operate deletion, or authorize fieldwork or restart. A named human must independently review and own any AI-assisted artifact before it can count as evidence.

## 3. Eligibility, exclusion, and screening

### Common eligibility floor

Every participant must:

- self-attest immediately before participation that they are at least 18 and currently located in California;
- demonstrate capacity to give informed, voluntary consent in the facilitator's judgment under the reviewed procedure;
- not be a member of the project team; and
- agree to the applicable activity after hearing the consent notice and correctly answering the comprehension checks.

### Protocol-specific eligibility

For **P0-04**, the participant must also:

- understand the roles of player and game master through fifth-edition fantasy tabletop experience, normally at least three sessions in the last two years;
- have wanted to play, scheduled play, played, or decided whether to play within the last 12 months; and
- be able to participate in English using text or speech, with a reasonable accommodation that can be supplied without asking for a diagnosis.

For **P0-05**, apply its own narrower eligibility rule without importing the P0-04 recency thresholds: the participant must be familiar enough with fifth-edition tabletop play to use the fixed pre-generated character, be able to consent, and not be a member of the project team. Prefer—but do not require—target-user participants who recently wanted to play when a human game master was unavailable. Recruit a mix of primarily-player and player/GM experience. Each of the minimum three qualifying P0-05 completions must come from a distinct eligible participant. Prior P0-04 or concept exposure is allowed only when tagged so familiarity bias remains visible.

### Exclusion and postponement

Exclude or postpone anyone who:

- is under 18, is not currently in California, or declines age/location self-attestation;
- is project staff, a protocol author, contractor, or has advance knowledge of intended findings;
- lacks the tabletop experience required to evaluate the target problem;
- is currently impaired, pressured, distressed before consent, or otherwise unable to consent;
- would need to disclose confidential employer, client, medical, legal, or similarly sensitive information to participate;
- cannot receive a required accommodation with the approved staff, time, or environment; or
- has a conflict that would make participation involuntary or reasonably feel tied to employment, grades, services, housing, medical care, or another consequential relationship.

Do not record the sensitive reason for exclusion. Use only a high-level disposition such as `ineligible-age/location`, `experience-floor`, `consent-capacity`, `conflict`, `accommodation-not-ready`, or `other-protocol`.

### Screening and sample controls

- For P0-04, use only its eight screener topics: adult/California status, recent fifth-edition experience, recent desire or attempt to play, player/GM role, remote/in-person context, rules era, prior generative-AI GM use, and requested accommodation without diagnosis.
- For P0-05, screen only adult/current-California status, consent ability, enough fifth-edition familiarity to use the pre-generated character, project-team or protocol conflict, requested accommodation without diagnosis, player/GM experience, prior concept/study exposure, and the non-required preferred target-user signal. Do not apply the P0-04 three-sessions/two-years or recent-12-month criteria as P0-05 exclusions.
- Keep answers brief at screening and stop collecting once eligibility and approved segment tags are known.
- Keep contact information only in the separate scheduling roster under a candidate code. Research notes use a different random study ID (`P04-__` or `P05-__`); only the restricted candidate-study lifecycle link joins the two codes.
- Recruit seven to nine P0-04 candidates to seek at least five eligible completions. Do not recruit only AI enthusiasts, project friends, or people favorable to the concept; no more than two of the minimum five may be close personal contacts of the owner or research lead.
- P0-04 completions must collectively meet its approved behavioral segmentation floor. Record a missing segment as missing; never infer it.
- P0-05 recruits as many eligible adults as necessary for three distinct qualifying completions and follows its full disposition rules. A withdrawal or non-completion is never replaced by pressure to continue.
- Recruitment is invitation-only. The frozen invitation must say the activity is unpaid, early research, voluntary, adults-only, and not a finished AI product. It must not promise fun, safety, therapeutic benefit, product access, or a favorable outcome.

## 4. Plain-language consent essentials

The approved notice and spoken opening must cover all items below in ordinary language. The participant receives or can retain a copy before deciding.

1. **Who and why:** identify the accountable operator and explain that this is early product research for a possible private alpha, not a sale or finished product.
2. **What happens:** distinguish a 50–60 minute P0-04 interview from a P0-05 paper prototype consisting of roughly 10–15 minutes of consent/control setup, 60–90 gameplay minutes, and 15–20 minutes of debrief. For P0-05, say plainly that humans use prewritten materials and manually apply rules/dice to simulate the proposed experience.
3. **Voluntary choice:** participation is optional. The person may skip a question or topic, pause, stop, withdraw, decline follow-up, or decline retention of not-yet-analyzed source material within the disclosed process without penalty or loss of an incentive. This study is unpaid.
4. **Reasonably foreseeable burdens:** time, possible boredom or frustration, disagreement about rules, discomfort with fictional content or questions, and residual recognition risk from a distinctive story, play choice, or short excerpt.
5. **Content ceiling:** Teen/PG-13; no explicit sexual content, sexual violence, hate-based targeting, encouragement of self-harm, or graphic torture/gore. P0-05 allows private lines/veils/themes-to-avoid without asking why.
6. **Data collected:** eligibility/segment tags, coded notes, observations, ratings, dispositions, optional participant text, and minimal report/incident operations as applicable. Identify anything a tool generates automatically.
7. **Data not requested:** names or contacts in research notes, exact address, employer, diagnosis, secrets, private messages, real campaign/member names, boundary reasons, or unnecessary incident narrative. Ask the participant not to disclose them.
8. **Coded, not anonymous:** explain the study ID and that removing direct identifiers does not guarantee anonymity or prevent recognition.
9. **No recording:** state that audio, video, screen, meeting transcription, automated summary, and full-transcript capture are prohibited for this round. A participant who asks to record must be asked not to; if recording cannot be stopped, the session does not begin or resumes only after it is disabled and consent is reconfirmed.
10. **Access and storage:** name the approved roles that can see each data class, the exact approved systems, and whether service providers, logs, sync, or backups exist.
11. **Dates and deletion:** state the exact contact-roster, boundary-sheet, token-lookup, raw-note, report/incident, synthesis, and backup dates or truthful limits; the deletion-request route and deadline; what linked material will be removed; what cannot be recalled; and what happens after lookup destruction.
12. **Excerpts and distribution:** identify the internal audience, use paraphrase by default, disclose that any short excerpt remains coded rather than anonymous, and state that no external distribution occurs before lookup destruction under this preflight.
13. **Controls and support limits:** explain pause, stop, and the human report route. State that reporting is not emergency or clinical support and staff are not therapists, clinicians, lawyers, emergency dispatchers, or ongoing monitors.
14. **Questions and contacts:** provide the research contact, privacy/deletion contact, actual-report recipient, and the reviewed safety/escalation information appropriate to this limited study.

### Consent decision and comprehension check

Before recording consent as `yes`, ask the participant to explain, in their own words:

- what activity they are joining and, for P0-05, what is human-simulated;
- that participation is unpaid and voluntary and they can skip, pause, stop, or withdraw;
- whether the session is recorded;
- what coded/not-anonymous means;
- how and by when they can request deletion; and
- how pause, stop, and a real report differ.

Correct misunderstandings neutrally and ask again. Do not coach a preferred answer. Record only `consent: yes/no`, notice version, date/time, facilitator, and comprehension status unless the qualified reviewer requires another method. A `no`, ambiguity, inability to explain the essentials after clarification, or use of an obsolete notice means the session does not start.

## 6. Deletion-token and withdrawal workflow

The privacy/data owner performs a dry run using synthetic records before recruitment and keeps the test evidence ID in the final gate.

1. At consent, generate the token, verify the participant can retain it privately, and explain the exact request route and deadline: **`REQUIRED: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM PT`**.
2. Store only the token hash-to-study-ID lookup in the restricted lookup location. Never place the readable token in the contact roster, research notes, report, incident record, or synthesis.
3. When a request arrives, restrict it to the approved deletion inbox and record only a request ID, receipt time, request type, and status. Redact unrelated narrative and incidental identifiers rather than copying them into the case record. Do not ask the participant to retell the interview, game, report, or incident.
4. Compare the submitted token through the approved process. If it does not match, disclose that the record cannot be located; request only a corrected token. Do not use identity questions or story details as substitute authentication unless a separately reviewed recovery process was disclosed. Remove the readable token from active messages and case notes immediately after matching, subject only to truthful provider-log limits.
5. Map the token to the study ID and immediately suspend new use or distribution of that participant's material. If the candidate-study link still exists, use it only to resolve the candidate code needed to find still-existing operations/provider artifacts; do not disclose contact identity to researchers.
6. Locate the source record and every linked evidence ID, excerpt, report, incident reference, editable synthesis passage, cached/exported working copy, still-existing roster or boundary record, deletion-inbox message, and reachable delivery/trash/audit/provider log covered by the notice.
7. Delete or transform each item exactly as promised. Remove linked excerpts from active notes and editable internal reports. Update aggregate counts without retaining a participant-specific reason or recreating the deleted link.
8. Check approved trash, version history, sync clients, and shared copies. For backups that cannot be immediately edited, record the disclosed latest purge date, prevent deliberate restoration into active use, and ensure restored systems reapply the deletion before use.
9. A second authorized person verifies the inventory and result without receiving more data than needed. Record the request ID, completion time, systems checked, exceptions already disclosed, and verifier—not the deleted content.
10. Confirm completion through the approved route within **`REQUIRED: N business days`** and no later than the team's disclosed commitment. Then purge the active confirmation/request conversation under its approved schedule; preserve only non-content delivery metadata if the provider cannot delete it and that limitation was disclosed. Escalate a missed or uncertain deletion as a privacy incident and halt new fieldwork.
11. At the approved candidate-link destruction time, verify that the linked roster and reachable communication/provider artifacts are already purged, destroy the candidate-code↔study-ID link, and record non-linkable completion metadata. At **`REQUIRED lookup-destruction date/time PT`**, destroy the token lookup, verify destruction, and retain only a non-linkable operational record that the destruction occurred. ## 8. Pause, stop, report, boundaries, and distress response

### Universal controls

- **Pause:** immediately halt questions or narration, dice, timers, note-taking beyond the minimum control event, and state progression. Ask only whether the participant wants a break, a permitted skip/fade/rewind, or to end. Resume only after an affirmative choice and an agreed restart point.
- **Stop:** immediately halt and end participation without requiring a reason. No question, roll, state update, or narrative continues. If the participant independently asks about options after the halt, staff may explain them without persuasion.
- **Real report:** bypasses the Wizard and reaches the named human recipient. Opening a report during any active activity first pauses it before collecting details. Collect only a coded session/turn ID, optional category, and optional text; acknowledge receipt and state recipient, access, follow-up posture, retention/deletion treatment, token route, and any disclosed mandatory escalation. Then ask whether to resume or stop. A post-session report does not change completed state.

The separate P0-04/P0-05 report-interface walkthrough uses neutral dummy content and never creates a fake real report. A real research-conduct concern may be raised through the same disclosed human route at any time. Reporting is not consent to continue and is not an emergency or clinical-support service.

For P0-05, collect boundary instructions without reasons on the separate sheet; use `configured/declined` in the session record. Never deliberately approach a boundary. Apply the fixed content ceiling even if a participant requests more intense content.

### Distress response

If a participant appears distressed, invokes a boundary, discloses an immediate concern, cannot affirmatively choose to resume, or any staff member believes safe participation is no longer possible:

1. halt questions/narration, dice, timers, recording if unexpectedly active, note-taking, and state immediately;
2. move research/game materials out of focus and say neutrally, “We have stopped the session. You do not need to explain.”;
3. ask only what is needed to choose among ending, a private break, contacting a trusted person, receiving the verified neutral resource sheet, or using the preapproved immediate-danger procedure;
4. do not diagnose, investigate, request proof, seek a detailed disclosure, press for a report, or promise confidentiality or emergency care beyond the notice;
5. follow the approved incident card if its reviewed threshold is met; staff must not improvise an emergency promise or resource; and
6. create only the minimum incident record and apply the participant's retention/deletion choice under the reviewed procedure.

Participation ends if the participant requests it, withdraws consent, cannot or does not affirmatively resume, remains distressed, staff cannot maintain the protocol or boundaries, a critical control/privacy/safety failure occurs, or the approved escalation procedure requires termination. A retained started session contributes to safety/disconfirming synthesis but never to the completion denominator unless it independently meets the protocol's full completion definition.

## 11. Environment, tool, and session checks

Before approving a location or platform, verify:

- a private, accessible setting without unapproved observers, smart speakers, cameras, or ambient recording;
- remote participants can privately self-attest that they are currently in California and can leave the session without staff intervention;
- platform recording, transcription, AI assistants, summaries, chat retention, and automatic cloud artifacts are disabled or truthfully included in the approved data map and notice;
- staff and participant display names do not unnecessarily expose legal names;
- notifications, unrelated tabs/documents, and personal accounts are hidden; screen sharing is limited to frozen study materials;
- only approved staff can enter; waiting-room or equivalent access control works; unexpected attendees cause a pause;
- the consent notice, control instructions, text, contrast, keyboard path, screen-reader path, and requested accommodations have been checked with the participant before the appointment where possible;
- a visible clock and separate stage timer work without recording content;
- `PAUSE`, `STOP`, and `REPORT` are visible and reachable independently of the Wizard; controls have a nontechnical fallback;
- the report recipient and safety primary/backup are reachable for the entire session window;
- when the P0-05 Wizard and rules operator are combined, the separately assigned trained rules-commitment auditor is present and the safety primary/backup remain separate and reachable;

- P0-05 dice/roller, commitment slips, state tracker, and correction log work offline or have an approved failure procedure; and

## 12. Recruitment, scheduling, and participant communications

1. Freeze and approve the invitation, screener, scheduling message, consent notice, reminder, cancellation, deletion acknowledgement, and follow-up templates.
2. Use only the approved invite list and recruitment period. Track invitation source so the final limitations statement can identify convenience sampling and relationship bias without naming people.
3. Send the consent notice early enough for review, but obtain live affirmative consent after age/location re-attestation and before substantive questions or prototype instructions.
4. Schedule the full activity plus a buffer; never rush consent, controls, distress response, or debrief to protect the calendar.
5. Ask about accommodations without requesting diagnosis. Confirm the supplied accommodation before the session; if it cannot be ready, reschedule or exclude under the approved non-sensitive category.
6. Reminders contain the unpaid/voluntary posture, duration, cancellation route, privacy expectation, and request to join from California in a private setting. Do not include study answers or sensitive screening details.
7. Do not ask for referrals or endorsement during the research portion. Store separately consent to later contact; absence of that consent means no research follow-up beyond operational/deletion communication.
8. Use separate disposition counts for invited, screened, eligible, consented, started, completed, participant-withdrew, facilitator-ended, safety-ended, timing/protocol-incomplete, deletion-requested, and evidence-retained.

## 13. Completion definitions and evidence integrity

### This preflight

The preflight is complete only when all exact values, signoffs, training records, environment checks, access tests, deletion tests, incident contacts, resource verification, and the final checklist are complete and internally consistent. Approval authorizes only the frozen research period and materials; it does not authorize production code, public launch, minors, payment, broader geography, recording, or new data uses.

### P0-04 fieldwork

P0-04 remains incomplete until its protocol's full done criteria are met, including at least five eligible consented completions meeting the segmentation floor; evidence in every required topic; balanced reveal-treatment exposure; coded records; hypothesis-linked synthesis; disconfirming evidence; honest limitations/saturation; honored deletion/withdrawal requests; and human A10 attestation that no result was invented. A favorable conversation, five recruits, or five starts is not completion.

### P0-05 fieldwork

P0-05 remains incomplete until its full done criteria are met and no critical issue remains unresolved, including at least three distinct eligible consented end-to-end completions. Each counted run must naturally play social, exploration, and exactly the accepted one required combat within the 60–90 minute gameplay envelope and reach an accepted success, failure, or recovery ending; prompted walkthroughs, safety terminations, softlocked/rescued runs, and timing failures remain evidence but not clean completions. Every consent-retained started session, including non-completions, appears in issue, safety, and disconfirming synthesis under its allowed disposition.

Never fabricate recruitment, consent, participant behavior, completion, signoff, deletion, dry-run, resource verification, or research results. Leave missing evidence missing.
