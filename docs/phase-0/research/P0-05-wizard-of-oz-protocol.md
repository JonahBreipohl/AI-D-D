## Purpose and limits

This protocol tests the proposed experience before software is built: whether a player can understand and complete the original 60–90 minute solo slice; whether the Transparent Guide, rules/ruling trace, dice visibility, content controls, and unsupported-action response feel coherent and fair; and where pacing, agency, confusion, rescue, or softlock failures occur.

The Wizard is a human simulation of a proposed interface, not an AI system and not evidence of model feasibility. The session may evaluate the product concept, adventure structure, and interaction contract. It cannot establish production latency, automated rules correctness, model safety, security, reliability, or unit economics.

## Roles and separation of authority

| Role | Responsibilities | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| Participant | Plays the pre-generated character, states intent in their own words, sets boundaries, and may pause, stop, or report at any time | Perform a scripted “correct” path or protect the team's desired result |
| Facilitator | Obtains consent, explains controls, keeps time, gives only standardized instructions, operates the out-of-band pause/stop, acknowledges reports, applies the incident procedure, and conducts the debrief | Coach a choice, defend the design, reveal hidden paths, probe for sensitive detail, or let schedule pressure override safety |
| Wizard / Transparent Guide | Uses the approved scene packet to narrate concisely, asks neutral clarification, offers optional choices, and narrates only after authoritative resolution | Pretend to be an implemented AI, alter rules/state, choose a roll result, expose GM-private facts, or invent support |
| Rules and dice operator | Selects the approved resolution lane, records the pre-roll commitment, applies the supported rule or bounded ruling, directs and records participant-initiated Rin rolls, initiates Watcher rolls visibly, and updates state | Initiate a Rin roll without an approved accessibility route, or change a target, modifier, consequence, or die after seeing the roll |
| Observer / note taker | Captures times, behavior, questions, errors, unsupported actions, rescues, softlocks, and coded excerpts without intervening | Interpret aloud, supply hints, copy boundary details, or become a second game master |

Use at least two research staff when practical. The facilitator may also observe. The Wizard may also operate rules only if every commitment is written before dice are touched and another staff member can audit it. Never combine the participant with a research role.

## Safe Wizard-of-Oz disclosure boundary

Read this before consent to play:

> This is an early paper prototype, not a finished product. A human facilitator will select or adapt responses from an approved adventure packet to simulate the proposed text experience, and another person may apply the rules and dice. Some story information will remain hidden as it would in a normal role-playing game. We are evaluating the experience and the design, not your skill. You may pause, skip a topic, stop, or use a separate human-routed report path at any time.

Permitted concealment is limited to ordinary game information: unrevealed scene facts, NPC motives, future branches, and which pre-authored response card the Wizard will use. The team may also avoid interrupting play to label every manual step, because the human simulation was disclosed up front.

The team must not:

- state or imply that an AI, rules engine, safety classifier, persistence system, or other unbuilt capability is operating;
- fabricate, reroll, choose, or secretly modify dice outcomes;
- change a committed ruling or consequence after seeing a roll;
- simulate a system failure, delay, or recovery and later report it as measured product behavior;
- claim an unsupported action worked through implemented mechanics;
- withhold the existence or operation of the separate out-of-band pause, stop, or report paths;
- expose a participant to undisclosed sensitive content to “test” the safety control; or
- misrepresent the purpose, data handling, incentive, or right to withdraw.

At debrief, state which parts were human-selected, prewritten, manually resolved, timed, or otherwise simulated. Record whether the disclosure changed the participant's interpretation. Any additional concealment requires separate qualified review and an explicit debrief plan; it is outside this protocol by default.

## Session shape and timing

The gameplay target is 75 minutes and must remain within the approved 60–90 minute slice. Consent, boundaries, control practice, and debrief are outside that gameplay timer.

| Stage | Target | Required content and observation |
|---|---:|---|
| Consent, boundaries, control practice | 10–15 min, outside game | Consent; line/veil/phobia choices; explain human simulation; participant activates pause/stop on a neutral practice prompt and separately walks through report submission/acknowledgement without inventing an incident |
| Hook and character orientation | 5 min | Original premise; pre-generated character; goal/stakes; optional actions are examples, not a menu limit |
| Social beat | 12–15 min | One meaningful NPC interaction with more than one supported approach and a consequence that carries forward |
| Exploration beat | 12–15 min | A clue, obstacle, hazard, or route decision using named zones/range bands where relevant; failure must not silently end progress |
| Combat encounter | 25–30 min | Play the one required bounded combat using initiative/action economy and the slice's declared features; position is expressed through zones/range bands; a run without this combat is not a counted completion |
| Ending or recovery | 8–10 min | Reach a success or failure ending, or visibly enter and resolve the defined recovery path; do not force a preferred ending |
| Debrief | 15–20 min, outside game | Comprehension, agency, fun, trust/fairness, pacing, unsupported actions, safety control, disclosure, and replay intent |

The facilitator marks actual start/end times per stage. If gameplay reaches 90 minutes without an ending, stop at the next safe boundary and record an incomplete timing failure; do not rush or invent an ending. If the participant reaches an ending before 60 minutes through legitimate choices, record it rather than padding the session.

## Start-of-session script

After general research consent and the Wizard-of-Oz disclosure, the facilitator says:

> You will play one short original solo adventure with this pre-generated character. You can describe any action in your own words; the examples are optional. The prototype supports a limited set of mechanics. If an action is outside that set, we will say so and help you express the same goal through supported options when possible rather than inventing a rule. Before a roll, we will show and commit the ruling, target, modifiers, advantage or disadvantage, and possible consequence. You initiate Rin's visible rolls; the rules/dice operator initiates the Watcher's visible rolls. We then apply the committed result and narrate only afterward. We do not secretly change results. If a system-state correction is needed, it will be visible; a discretionary rewind happens only with your agreement. You can pause or stop immediately at any time without explaining why. You can also report an issue to [human recipient role]; opening a report during play pauses first, and after acknowledgement you choose whether to resume or stop.

Replace the bracketed human recipient and all other operational placeholders before the session. Ask the participant to explain the flow in their own words and record misunderstandings before clarifying. Then run the neutral pause/stop practice: the Wizard immediately stops speaking, the rules operator touches nothing, and the facilitator confirms whether to resume or end. Separately walk through a dummy report with no real incident detail, verify that active-play reporting pauses before collection and that acknowledgement/recipient/access/deletion information is clear, then let the participant choose resume or stop. These controls are not tests of the participant and must not contain boundary-sensitive material.

## Turn and ruling procedure

For every consequential player intent:

1. **Intent:** The Wizard repeats the goal in neutral language and asks at most one necessary clarification.
2. **Lane:** The rules operator labels the resolution `deterministic`, `bounded pre-roll ruling`, or `unsupported/clarify`.
3. **Commitment and prototype disclosure:** Before any roll, write the turn ID, actor, action, target, rule or ruling basis, DC/defense, modifiers, advantage/disadvantage, resources, possible success/failure consequences, and visibility, then place every non-hidden committed field face-up. Hidden facts remain sealed and cannot be selected after the roll. This is the accepted P0-05 prototype convention, not the final Phase 2 policy.
4. **Opportunity to question:** Let the participant inspect the face-up record, ask for a rule explanation, or dispute the interpretation before rolling. Clarification does not cost an action.
5. **Roll:** Roll in the open. Announce and record notation, each raw die, selected die, modifiers, and total.
6. **Reveal and apply:** Reveal any previously sealed hidden field only when its accepted visibility rule permits. The rules operator updates state and the event tracker before narration.
7. **Narrate:** Only now does the Wizard narrate the committed outcome without changing its mechanical meaning.
8. **Audit/correction:** If an error is found, pause. A mechanically invalid update receives a visible correction/compensation under P0-03. A discretionary rewind is proposed visibly and occurs only after the solo participant affirmatively agrees. Never erase the original ledger entry or reseal a replacement as if it were original.

Any mercy is offered openly before the roll and logged as a changed consequence, advantage, reroll resource, or other P0-03-supported choice. There is no secret fudging.

## Unsupported-action procedure and log

The Wizard must not silently coerce an unsupported intent into a different action or invent a mechanic. Use this response:

> I understand that your goal is **[neutral restatement]**. This prototype does not support **[specific mechanic or scope]**. You can **[supported option one]**, **[supported option two]**, or tell me more about the goal so we can see whether it maps to a supported action.

If the intent is ordinary nonmechanical role-play already allowed by P0-03, do not label it unsupported merely because no roll is needed. If the participant's goal cannot be preserved by an in-scope option, say so plainly and record it.

Create one entry for every unsupported or remapped intent:

```text
Session / turn / scene:
Coded player intent (near-verbatim, with incidental identifiers removed):
Stated player goal:
Resolution lane selected:
Why unsupported or why clarification was required:
Options offered:
Participant reaction: understood / disappointed / challenged / abandoned goal / other
Time to resume progress:
Did the goal remain achievable? yes / partly / no
Did staff provide non-scripted help? yes/no; rescue ID if yes
Candidate disposition: add coverage / improve affordance / improve explanation / remain unsupported
Evidence class: observed natural play / prompted walkthrough
```

## Content boundaries and out-of-band controls

Before play, the participant may list lines (exclude entirely), veils (allow only off-screen), and phobias or themes to avoid. Do not ask why. Put only the minimum operational instruction on the separate boundary sheet; never copy the participant's personal history into it or the session record. Delete the sheet on its precommitted early date as described above. The fixed Teen/PG-13 ceiling applies even if a participant requests more intense content.

### Pause and stop

`PAUSE` and `STOP` are out of band and bypass the Wizard. When either is activated verbally or through its visible control:

1. the Wizard stops immediately, even mid-sentence;
2. no roll, state update, timer pressure, or narrative generation proceeds;
3. for pause, the facilitator asks only whether the participant wants a break, a skip/fade/rewind within the accepted safety contract, or to end; for stop, participation ends unless the participant independently asks to discuss options after the halt;
4. no reason is required and the participant's incentive is unaffected;
5. resumption requires the participant's affirmative choice and a stated safe restart point; and
6. the log records only the control outcome and design issue needed for research, not sensitive personal detail.

A stop bypass, delayed halt, boundary violation, or state change after activation is a critical failure. End or pause the study round, protect the participant, and review before another session. Never deliberately approach a participant's boundary to create a test case.

### Report

`REPORT` is a separate out-of-band human route for a product, fairness, privacy, safety, or research-conduct concern. It does not rely on or send content through the Wizard. It is not an emergency or clinical-support service.

1. Opening report attaches only coded session/turn ID and offers one optional category plus optional free text. It does not attach raw transcript, backstory, contact data, boundary detail, or the full session by default.
2. The facilitator acknowledges receipt without asking for a narrative: “Your report was received by [human role]. You do not need to add detail now.”
3. Opening a report during active play pauses narration, dice, timers, and state progression before collection. After acknowledgement, the flow asks whether to resume or stop. It never silently resumes or ends the session. A post-session report does not alter completed state.
4. The acknowledgement names the receiving role, allowed access group, expected follow-up posture, exact retention/deletion treatment, deletion-token route, and any mandatory escalation disclosed during consent.
5. During pre-session control practice, walk through a dummy report and acknowledgement with no real incident. Record comprehension; do not create a fake incident record.
6. For a real report, preserve only the minimum participant-supplied detail and operational classification needed to route it. Store it separately with more restrictive access when it contains safety, privacy, research-conduct, or boundary information.

Failure to acknowledge or route a report as disclosed, overcollection, access outside the named group, or treating report as consent to continue is a protocol issue and may be critical depending on consequence.

### Staff support limits and distress/incident response

Research staff provide process control and immediate study safety only. They are not acting as therapists, clinicians, crisis counselors, legal advisers, emergency dispatchers, or ongoing monitors, and must not promise clinical assessment, confidentiality beyond the notice, or crisis care.

Before recruitment, the human safety owner must approve a short incident card, termination criteria, escalation contacts, and a neutral resource sheet appropriate to the study location. A human verifies every resource immediately before the study round; do not rely on an unverified number or imply endorsement.

If the participant appears distressed, invokes a boundary, discloses an immediate concern, or staff believe safe participation is no longer possible:

1. halt narration, dice, timers, and state immediately;
2. move the game materials out of focus and use a neutral statement such as, “We have stopped the session. You do not need to explain.”;
3. ask only what is necessary to choose among ending participation, taking a private break, contacting a person the participant trusts, receiving the neutral resource sheet, or following the preapproved emergency/escalation procedure;
4. do not diagnose, investigate, request proof, or press for a report;
5. if the preapproved immediate-danger threshold is met, follow that reviewed procedure and applicable obligations; staff must not improvise an emergency promise; and
6. create a separate minimum-necessary incident record with coded ID, time, control used, operational action, recipients, and disposition—not sensitive narrative.

Participation ends when the participant requests it; withdraws consent; cannot or does not affirmatively choose to resume after a pause; remains distressed; the facilitator cannot maintain the accepted protocol or boundaries; a critical safety/privacy/control failure occurs; or the preapproved escalation procedure requires termination. Incentive handling follows the consent notice and is not contingent on explaining the event. A retained started session remains in the issue/safety/disconfirming synthesis under the disposition rules, but never in the completion denominator.

## Observation events and rubric

The observer records time-stamped behavior before interpretation. Standard event codes are:

- `HES` — participant hesitates for at least 10 seconds after enough information is available;
- `ASK` — asks what actions are possible or what the system expects;
- `MIS` — states a materially incorrect understanding of state, zone, rule, consequence, or objective;
- `DIS` — disputes a ruling, die, correction, or fairness outcome;
- `UNA` — attempts an unsupported action;
- `REMAP` — accepts or rejects a supported reframing;
- `WAIT` — comments on, disengages during, or reacts to response delay;
- `TRACE` — opens/asks for or ignores a ruling/dice/history artifact;
- `BOUND` — sets or invokes a content boundary;
- `STOP` — uses pause/stop, including the neutral practice;
- `REPORT` — opens, submits, or discusses the report path, including the neutral walkthrough;
- `INCIDENT` — staff invoke the distress/incident procedure or end participation for safety;
- `RESCUE` — receives nonstandard progress-enabling staff help; and
- `LOCK` — structural or usability softlock as defined below.

At the end of each scene, score each dimension from 0 to 3 and attach at least one observation ID. Scores without behavioral evidence are invalid.

| Dimension | 3 — smooth | 2 — minor friction | 1 — material failure | 0 — critical/blocking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal and next-action clarity | Understands goal and acts without help | One clarification or brief hesitation | Repeated confusion or strong coaching pressure | Cannot progress without rescue |
| Agency | Multiple meaningful intents feel available | One avoidable constraint | Feels railroaded or abandons a goal after remap | No meaningful supported choice |
| Rules/dice comprehension | Predicts the committed flow and result | Needs one explanation | Misunderstands despite explanation or trace is unusable | Outcome cannot be audited or commitment changes post-roll |
| Fairness/trust | Expresses confidence grounded in behavior | Mild concern resolved by evidence | Persistent distrust or disputed correction | Secret manipulation, unresolvable dispute, or participant refuses resolution |
| Pacing | Engaged; scene fits target | Brief drag/rush without loss of control | Repeated disengagement or scene materially misses target | Session cannot reach an ending inside 90 minutes |
| Unsupported-action recovery | Goal maps cleanly or clear limit is accepted | Minor disappointment, progress resumes | Goal lost, repeated unsupported attempts, or >2 minutes to resume | Unsupported behavior causes rescue or softlock |
| Spatial/state clarity | Correctly understands zone, resources, and current state | One correction | Repeated misconception affects choices | State is contradictory or unusable |
| Boundary/control confidence | Distinguishes pause, stop, and report; controls and boundaries work as disclosed | One clarification | Conflates controls, hesitates to use them, or handling creates concern | Boundary violation, stop bypass, post-stop state change, report mishandling, or unsafe continuation |

Also record the actual duration of every scene; number of unsupported intents, rules questions, disputes, corrections, rescues, and softlocks; and whether each ending/recovery path was naturally observed or only walked through.

## Rescue and softlock definitions

### Rescue

A **rescue** occurs when staff provide progress-enabling help outside the accepted run sheet, such as revealing a hidden path, naming the correct choice, changing a rule or state, waiving an obstacle, inventing a new affordance, retconning an outcome, or moving the participant to the next scene.

These are not rescues when delivered as pre-scripted and available to every participant: repeating already public information; explaining the controls or an approved rule; supplying a requested accessibility accommodation; applying the explicit unsupported-action response; or invoking the safety procedure. Log those separately. If uncertain, classify it as a rescue and explain.

### Softlock

- A **structural softlock** exists when, in the current valid state, no supported action or declared transition can reach a success, failure, recovery, or ending without changing the approved rules, content, or state.
- A **usability softlock** exists when a valid route exists but the participant cannot identify or execute it after three minutes and one standardized neutral reorientation prompt: “Please tell me your current goal and what information or action you think is available.”
- A **near-softlock** is recovered without nonstandard help but requires two failed good-faith attempts, more than two minutes of confusion, or repeated incorrect state understanding. It is not counted as a softlock, but it must be revised or explicitly accepted with rationale.

When a softlock occurs, log the exact state and attempts, then rescue the participant only with their agreement so the remainder may continue. The session remains valuable evidence but is marked `softlock: yes` and cannot be represented as an unassisted completion.

## Post-session questions and ratings

Ask in this order before explaining design intent:

1. “Tell me what happened in the adventure in your own words.”
2. “What did you think your goal was? Did it change?”
3. “Where did you feel most and least free to act?”
4. “When were you unsure what to do or what the game state meant?”
5. “Which ruling or dice result did you trust most? Which did you trust least? What made the difference?”
6. “How did seeing the check, target, modifiers, possible consequence, raw roll, and result affect play?”
7. “How did the no-secret-fudging and visible-correction policy feel?”
8. “What happened when an action was unsupported? Did the response preserve your goal?”
9. “How did the pace feel in each scene? Where did you wait or feel rushed?”
10. “Could you find and distinguish pause and stop? What did you expect each to do?”
11. “Without describing a real concern, show me how you would report one. Who receives it, what would be attached, and what acknowledgement, access, retention, deletion, or follow-up would you expect?”
12. “Would reporting also pause or stop play, ask you which you want, or leave play unchanged? What did this prototype do?”
13. “What, if anything, crossed or approached a content boundary?” Do not request personal explanation.
14. “What was the most enjoyable moment? The least enjoyable?”
15. “On a 1–5 scale, how much fun was this session? What makes it that number?”
16. “On a 1–5 scale, how fair did the game feel? What makes it that number?”
17. “On a 1–5 scale, how much did you trust the outcomes? What makes it that number?”
18. “Would you voluntarily play another session in this format: yes, maybe, or no? Why?”
19. “What is the one change you would make first?”
20. “What is the strongest reason this experience should not be built?”

Then debrief the human simulation, prewritten content, manual rules/dice/state handling, any timed delays, and any prompted walkthrough. Ask: “Does knowing what was simulated change any answer you gave?” Record the response without persuasion.

## Three-session sequence

The minimum round uses a deliberate learn-and-retest sequence, not three identical demonstrations:

1. **Session 1 — Baseline:** Run the accepted materials without mid-session design changes. Identify comprehension, pacing, boundary-control, unsupported-action, rescue, and softlock failures.
2. **Session 2 — Targeted revision:** Apply only documented changes approved under the revision rules. Preserve unchanged parts so the comparison is interpretable. If Session 1 did not exercise recovery, use a predeclared state walkthrough after natural play rather than rigging the live outcome.
3. **Session 3 — Regression and alternate path:** Use a fresh participant. Confirm critical/major repairs and encourage a different supported approach through neutral starting context or optional choices, never by directing the participant. Recheck any failure/recovery path not yet evidenced.

Additional sessions are required when a critical issue occurs, Session 3 introduces a new structural change, fewer than three participants complete end to end without a safety termination, or the last material revision has not been tested by a fresh participant.

## Revision and stopping rules

Freeze materials within a session. Safety action is immediate, but product changes happen only between versions and appear in the change log with source observation IDs.

### Contract classification before severity

Classify every proposed change first as `within accepted contract` or `changes accepted contract`.

- A within-contract change may adjust wording, card layout, timing prompts, note-taking mechanics, or facilitation clarity without altering the behavior accepted in P0-03.
- A contract-changing proposal includes any change to the pre-generated build or capabilities; supported/unsupported rule envelope; social, exploration, or combat pillars; the requirement for one combat per counted run; topology; entry/exit or ending/recovery semantics; persona; authority; ruling-commitment or reveal timing; no-secret-fudging, mercy, correction, or consent policy; content ceiling, boundaries, pause/stop/report, or other fairness/safety behavior; duration; or any other accepted P0-03/interface contract.

A10 and A5 may recommend a contract change but may not approve it inside P0-05. Stop affected sessions, return the proposal and evidence to A0 plus the human owner, revise and reaccept P0-03, create a new major run-pack version and crosswalk, and state which earlier evidence is no longer comparable. An immediate protective action may be taken during a safety incident, but it does not authorize resuming counted sessions with an unaccepted contract.

| Severity | Definition | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Boundary violation or stop bypass; post-stop state change; secret roll/ruling manipulation; report mishandling with material consequence; unsafe continuation; no reachable ending/recovery; participant distress attributable to protocol; material consent/privacy failure | Stop the study round, apply participant-protective/incident steps, review with A10/A5 and the appropriate safety/privacy owner, route contract changes to A0 + human, then rerun affected evidence with a fresh participant only after approval |
| Major | Rescue or softlock; repeated loss of agency; persistent distrust; core scene outside timing; unsupported action destroys the player's goal; rules/dice trace cannot be understood | Revise before the next counted session and explicitly regression-test the change |
| Moderate | Repeated hesitation, misleading affordance, remap friction, avoidable rules explanation, or pacing drag that does not block completion | Revise or record a reason to test unchanged in the next session |
| Minor | Isolated wording, layout, or facilitation issue with no material effect | Batch after the session; preserve for later verification |

Revision controls:

- A10 owns the evidence classification; A5 owns adventure revisions. Neither may erase contrary evidence.
- Every change names the source session/observation, hypothesis, expected effect, affected materials, and next test.
- Do not change a success metric, rescue definition, softlock definition, or score anchor after seeing results.
- Do not generalize one participant's preference into a requirement unless it exposes a safety, accessibility, consent, or structural blocker. Record an alternative hypothesis and seek corroboration.
- Apply the contract classification before editing any run material; a major version number does not substitute for A0 + human reacceptance.
- If gameplay exceeds 90 minutes twice for the same structural reason, or two participants require rescue at the same node, stop adding facilitator workarounds and return the slice to P0-03/A5 redesign.

## Done evidence

P0-05 is complete only when all of the following exist and no critical issue remains unresolved:

- a full disposition flow reports invited, screened, eligible, consented, started, completed, withdrawal/termination/incompletion categories, deletion requests, and evidence retained, with reasons only at a non-sensitive category level;
- every consent-retained started session appears in issue, safety, and disconfirming-evidence synthesis, while the completion denominator is reported separately;
- at least three distinct eligible, consenting participants meet the fixed end-to-end completion definition, with more sessions run as necessary to obtain three completions;
- all started sessions used accepted, versioned, provenance-safe materials with an accepted P0-03 crosswalk and identify every change between versions;
- each counted completion naturally played the social and exploration pillars and the one required combat within the 60–90 minute gameplay envelope; a walkthrough or attempted-but-unreached combat does not qualify;
- per-stage timing, behavioral events, scene rubric, available post-session ratings, and replay intent are retained as coded/pseudonymous evidence, with missing data left missing rather than inferred;
- every consequential roll has a complete face-up pre-roll commitment for all non-hidden fields, sealed handling for hidden fields, and a visible dice/outcome record, with no unexplained change after commitment;
- every started participant configured or declined boundaries; boundary details were stored separately/minimally and deleted on the early schedule; and every participant who reached controls successfully exercised neutral pause/stop practice and the separate report walkthrough, with any live use recorded without sensitive detail;
- the report evidence records minimal collection, acknowledgement, recipient/access, follow-up posture, retention/deletion, deletion-token route, and relationship to pause/stop;
- all unsupported or remapped actions are logged with whether the player's goal remained achievable and how progress resumed;
- rescues, structural softlocks, usability softlocks, near-softlocks, corrections, and disputes are explicitly counted under the fixed definitions;
- success, failure, and recovery paths are each marked as naturally observed, walked through from a predeclared state, or not tested—never implied;
- a revision log traces every change to evidence, classifies contract impact, links every accepted-contract change to A0 + human reacceptance, and shows that every critical/major repair was retested with a fresh participant;
- the final synthesis separates observed natural play, prompted walkthroughs, participant opinions, and researcher interpretation and includes negative evidence from retained non-completions;
- unresolved design risks and proposed updates are handed to P0-03, P0-06, P0-08, P0-10, and P0-14 as applicable; and
- A10 and A5 sign the evidence packet and explicitly state that the sessions do not demonstrate production AI, rules-engine, latency, safety-automation, or security capability.
