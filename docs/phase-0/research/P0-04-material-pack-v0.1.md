## 4. Screener and disposition form

### 4.1 Administration rules

- Use only after the P0-04 preflight says `GO` and only within the approved invitation/screening period.
- Keep identity and contact details in the restricted scheduling roster. The research corpus receives only an approved candidate code and, after consent, a separately generated study ID.

### 4.2 Exact screener questions

1. Are you 18 or older and currently located in California?
2. About how many fifth-edition fantasy tabletop sessions have you played in the last two years?
3. In the last 12 months, have you wanted or attempted to play? What happened? Please keep this to one or two sentences.
4. In those sessions, were you mainly a player, mainly a game master, or both?
5. Was your recent play mainly remote, mainly in person, or mixed?
6. Which rules era did your group mainly use, if you know: 2014-era, revised 2024, mixed, or not sure?
7. Have you used a generative-AI game-master or solo role-playing tool before?
8. What accessibility accommodation, if any, would help you participate? Please describe the participation need, not a diagnosis.

### 4.4 Eligibility and exclusion decision rule

An eligible candidate must self-attest that they are 18 or older and currently in California; be able to give informed, voluntary consent; have enough fifth-edition fantasy tabletop experience to understand player and game-master roles, normally at least three sessions in the last two years; have wanted to play, scheduled play, or decided whether to play in the last 12 months; and be able to participate in English through text or speech with a reasonable, approved accommodation if requested.

Exclude project staff, protocol authors, contractors, anyone with advance knowledge of intended findings, anyone without sufficient relevant play experience, anyone currently unable to consent, and anyone whose participation would require disclosure of confidential employer, client, medical, legal, or similarly sensitive information. Also exclude or postpone anyone whose participation could reasonably feel tied to employment, grades, services, housing, medical care, or another consequential relationship. Any `UNCLEAR` administrative conflict result is `INCOMPLETE` and cannot be scheduled until a named human A10 review resolves it. Prior project participation must be tagged rather than silently ignored. No more than two of the minimum five completions may be close personal contacts of the owner or research lead. Record only an approved non-sensitive disposition code; do not diagnose or speculate.

### `P04-CONCEPT-01` — exact card

> Imagine an invite-only, text-first tool for adults that runs one original 60–90 minute, fifth-edition-compatible solo adventure with a pre-generated character. A “Transparent Guide” narrates and offers optional choices, but deterministic rules—not the narrator—apply game state. The experience commits rulings before rolls and provides dice and audit evidence; this interview will compare two ways of revealing that committed information. It does not secretly change rolls, labels unsupported actions, and provides separate out-of-band pause, stop, and report controls. This is a concept, not a description of a completed product.

Immediate neutral prompts:

1. “In your own words, what do you think this would and would not do?”
2. “When, if ever, would this fit into how you play?”
3. “What is the strongest reason you would not try it?”
4. “How does a short solo slice compare with the session you actually want?”
5. “Would use of revised 2024 rules change your decision? Why?”

Target duration remains 50–60 minutes. The exact scheduled duration in participant communications must be reconciled with the approved notice.

| Stage | Target | Material | Moderator constraint |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Live consent and context | 5 min | Approved notice, `P04-CON-01` | Do not begin substantive collection without comprehension pass and affirmative consent |
| Recent behavior and GM access | 10–12 min | Protocol prompts | Ask for the last real example; never offer “no GM” as the answer |
| Concept comprehension and fit | 5 min | `P04-CONCEPT-01` | Record misunderstanding before minimum factual clarification |

| Latency and pacing | 5–7 min | Reviewed latency storyboard | Use assigned order; record behavior/assumptions by task |

| Payment and alternatives | 5–7 min | Protocol prompts | Ask last; treat price answers as directional, never as a forecast |
| Close | 3 min | Close prompts and deletion reminder | Ask separately about later contact; do not solicit endorsement or referrals |

### 9.1 Opening and transition language

- Start after consent: “To begin, tell me about the last tabletop role-playing session you played.”
- Neutral probe: “Tell me more.” / “What happened next?” / “What makes you say that?”
- Concept transition: “I will now show the same short concept card used for each conversation.”
- Treatment transition: “You will see two presentations of the same fixed event. Only when information is revealed changes.”
- Sorting transition: “There is no correct arrangement. Place each concept where you would expect it, and explain your reasoning.”
- Close: “What is the biggest assumption in this idea that you think we have wrong?” / “What single event would make you stop using it?” / “What did I not ask that matters?”

### 9.2 Neutrality and halt card

- Do not defend the concept, owner, rules edition, price, design work, or preferred answer.
- Do not turn confusion into enthusiasm or contrary evidence into a feature request.
- Ask about observed recent behavior before hypotheticals. Separate a participant's comprehension from preference.
- Ask one issue at a time. Do not combine “fun and fair,” “fast and accurate,” or any other compound question; split it and preserve the response order.
- Never say another participant agreed. Never infer a diagnosis, protected characteristic, or motive.
- Complete the strongest-disconfirming-evidence field before discussing findings with the owner, designers, or anyone who has a stake in a favorable result.
- If sensitive or identifying detail appears, interrupt gently, ask the participant not to continue that detail, and paraphrase only the minimum non-identifying research point if retention is permitted.
- `PAUSE` immediately halts questions and note-taking beyond the minimal control event. `STOP` ends the activity without requiring a reason. A real `REPORT` follows the approved human route and pauses first during active research.
- Distress, persistent consent misunderstanding, unavailable accountable staff, unexpected recording/bot, unauthorized observer, privacy failure, material protocol defect, or inability to maintain the approved accommodation is a stop condition under the preflight.

### 9.3 Core prompt checklist

Use the exact concept, reveal, latency, interface, report, and close prompts already printed in sections 6–8 and 9.1. Use these remaining prompts without implying a preferred answer.

**Opening and recent behavior**

1. “To start, tell me about the last tabletop role-playing session you played.”
2. “What role did you have, and what rules era or tools did the group use?”
3. “What makes a session worth the effort for you?”
4. “Tell me about the most recent time you wanted to play but the session did not happen, or happened later than you wanted.”
5. “Walk me through how the group tried to organize it.”
6. “What prevented or delayed play?” Do not offer “no GM” as an answer.
7. “What did you do instead?”
8. “How often has something like that happened in the last six months?” Accept ranges and uncertainty.
9. “Who normally game-masters, and what happens when that person is unavailable or does not want to prepare?”
10. “Have you tried solo play, rotating game masters, prewritten tools, virtual tabletops, chatbots, or another substitute? What worked and failed?”
11. “If a substitute game master were available immediately, when would you use it, if at all?”

Probe without assuming the cause: scheduling, preparation burden, campaign commitment, group availability, social preference, rules confidence, and content access.

**Payment and alternatives — ask only after all other concept tasks**

1. “What, if anything, do you currently pay for to make tabletop play possible or easier?”
2. “Who usually pays: one host, each player, or someone else?”
3. “If this concept worked as described, what would it replace, and what would it not replace?”
4. “In what situation would you consider paying for it, if any?”
5. “Which payment shape would make the most sense or least sense—per adventure, a host subscription, a group subscription, or something else? Why?”
6. Only after those answers: “At what price would you dismiss it as too expensive? At what price would you question whether it could be good enough? What price would feel plausible?”
7. “What would you use if this remained free but limited? What would you use if it were unavailable?”

Treat price responses as directional hypotheses, not forecasts. Do not average five hypothetical price points into a market estimate.
