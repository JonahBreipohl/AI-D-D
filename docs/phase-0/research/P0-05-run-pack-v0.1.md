## 4. Session sequence and timing card

Consent, boundaries, control practice, and debrief are outside the gameplay timer. Start the gameplay clock at the first actionable in-world prompt; stop it at a terminal result.

| Stage | Target | Required evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Consent, boundaries, control practice | 10–15 min outside game | Valid consent; boundary configured/declined; neutral pause/stop practice; separate dummy report walkthrough |
| Hook and character orientation | 5 min | Premise, Rin reference, goal/stakes, examples described as optional |
| Fogbound Quay — social | 12–15 min | Meaningful NPC interaction; more than one supported approach; state carried forward |
| Broken Sluice — exploration | 12–15 min | Clue/route and traversal resolution; failure-forward behavior |
| Dark Lantern — combat | 25–30 min | The one required combat actually played; initiative/action economy; zone state |
| First Light — ending/recovery | 8–10 min | Exactly one accepted terminal in-fiction outcome unless play ended for safety |
| Debrief | 15–20 min outside game | Comprehension, agency, pacing, trust/fairness, controls, disclosure, replay intent |

At 90 gameplay minutes without an ending, stop at the next safe boundary and record `timing-incomplete`; do not rush or invent an ending. If legitimate play ends before 60 minutes, record the actual duration; do not pad it. A counted completion must naturally include social, exploration, and exactly one combat, then reach an accepted ending or recovery within 60–90 gameplay minutes and complete the core debrief.

## 6. Player control cards

These controls are visible, reachable, keyboard/screen-reader equivalent, and out of band. They bypass the Wizard.

### Card C1 — PAUSE

> **PAUSE** — Stop narration, dice, timers, and state now. I do not need to give a reason. Nothing resumes unless I explicitly choose to resume and agree on a safe restart point. I may instead choose a break, an accepted skip/fade/rewind, or to end.

Operator response: stop immediately, even mid-sentence. Touch no dice or state. Ask only whether the participant wants a break, an accepted skip/fade/rewind, or to end. Do not infer consent to resume.

### Card C2 — STOP

> **STOP** — End play now. Stop narration, dice, timers, and state. I do not need to give a reason, and my incentive is unaffected.

Operator response: stop immediately. Participation ends unless the participant independently asks to discuss options after the halt. Do not convert stop into pause or ask for justification.

### Card C3 — REPORT

> **REPORT** — Open a separate human route for a product, fairness, privacy, safety, or research-conduct concern. During active play, reporting pauses narration, dice, timers, and state before anything is collected. I may provide an optional category and optional text. After acknowledgement, I choose whether to resume or stop. A post-session report does not change completed game state.

The report mockup may attach only coded session/turn ID by default. It must not attach a transcript, backstory, contact data, boundary detail, or full session. Required acknowledgement:

> Your report was received by **[BLOCKING — approved human recipient role]**. You do not need to add detail now. **[BLOCKING — disclose allowed access group, follow-up posture, exact retention/deletion treatment, deletion-token route, and any reviewed mandatory escalation.]**

Do not field while either acknowledgement placeholder remains. REPORT is not an emergency or clinical-support service.

### Neutral control practice

Before gameplay:

1. Ask the participant to activate PAUSE or STOP on a neutral prompt; the Wizard stops, the rules operator touches nothing, and the facilitator confirms the resulting choice.
2. Separately walk through a dummy REPORT with no real incident detail. Confirm that active-play reporting pauses before collection, acknowledgement information is understandable, and resume/stop remains explicit.
3. Record comprehension; do not create a fake incident record or use boundary-sensitive content.

Practice prompt text and the accessible control presentation are `[BLOCKING — human preflight and accessibility dry run]`; staff may not invent them with a participant present.

## 7. Transparent Guide behavior card

- Be concise, beginner-friendly, neutral in rules strictness, and explicit that the declared threat is medium.
- Explain a rule on request. Label the lane as deterministic, bounded pre-roll ruling, or unsupported/clarify.
- Offer examples as optional choices, never a closed menu unless the accepted rules truly allow only those options.
- Vary wording and pacing only. Do not vary mechanics, fact visibility, committed consequences, dice, state, or correction consent.
- Ask at most one neutral clarification when ambiguity would change actor, target, rule, cost, visibility, or consequence.
- Narrate only after the rules operator applies the committed outcome to state.
- Never coach toward a preferred route or ending, secretly rescue, secretly show mercy, alter a roll, or invent a fact/mechanic.
- If the participant uses PAUSE, STOP, REPORT, or a boundary, stop speaking immediately and yield to the facilitator.
- If required card content is absent or a rule is not on the provisional pre-session P0-06 operator sheet, stop the resolution. Do not improvise.
